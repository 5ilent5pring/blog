Dmitry Peskov's recent rhetoric regarding Ukrainian drone strikes on civilian infrastructure deliberately excludes the massive May 17 attack on Moscow and the Moscow region. This selective framing attempts to decouple the Kremlin's ongoing offensive in Ukraine, such as the May 14 strike that killed 24 people in Kyiv, from the retaliatory kinetic actions occurring within Russian territory. The Kremlin is likely attempting to maintain a narrative of victimhood to justify continued operations while avoiding an explicit admission of domestic vulnerability that could trigger domestic unrest.

## Scenario Analysis

- The conflict will transition into a period of high-intensity, localized kinetic strikes with no significant diplomatic breakthrough.
  - The expiration of the May 9-11 US-brokered truce and the subsequent Russian resumption of attacks on Kyiv suggest that the 'pause' mentioned by Peskov is a temporary tactical lull rather than a strategic shift. If Russia continues its current trajectory of targeting Ukrainian urban centers, Ukraine will likely respond with increased deep-strike drone capabilities against Russian logistics and civilian hubs. This scenario is supported by the recent May 14 casualties in Kyiv. A reversal would require a documented return to the negotiation frameworks mentioned by Peskov in March.
  - *most likely*
- The United States will successfully leverage its diplomatic position to enforce a sustained, multi-week ceasefire.
  - The Kremlin's explicit reliance on 'American colleagues' to continue peacekeeping efforts indicates a strategic dependency on US mediation to stabilize the front. If the US administration can move beyond the failed three-day May truce to a more robust monitoring mechanism, the current cycle of drone-led retaliation may decelerative. This is inconsistent with the recent escalation in drone strikes on Moscow and the Moscow region. A reversal would be the total withdrawal of US diplomatic involvement in the mediation process.
  - *less likely but significant*
- A rapid escalation occurs where drone strikes on Moscow trigger a massive, uncoordinated Russian retaliatory strike on Ukrainian energy infrastructure.
  - The scale of the May 17 attack on Moscow and the Moscow region represents a threshold of domestic disruption that could force the Kremlin to abandon the 'peace process' rhetoric entirely. Such an escalation would move the conflict from localized border skirmishes to a systematic campaign against the Ukrainian power grid. This is supported by the increasing frequency of drone incursions into Russian-held territory. A reversal would be a period of prolonged stability in the Moscow region despite continued Ukrainian drone activity.
  - *low probability but high impact*

## Key Assumptions Check

- The Kremlin views the May 17 Moscow drone attack as a manageable tactical event rather than a strategic failure of air defense.
  - Peskov's failure to mention the Moscow/Moscow region attack while complaining about other 'civilian targets' suggests the Kremlin is not yet ready to signal a loss of domestic control. If the Kremlin begins to officially acknowledge these strikes in press briefings, it would indicate that the domestic political cost of the attack has exceeded their threshold for controlled information. A reversal would be the emergence of widespread, uncurated civilian reports of significant damage in Moscow that the Kremlin cannot ignore.
  - *almost certainly*
- US-led mediation remains the primary diplomatic lever for de-escalation in the current conflict phase.
  - Peskov's specific call for 'American colleagues' to continue peacekeeping efforts demonstrates that the Kremlin still views US involvement as a functional component of the negotiation architecture. If the US shifts toward a purely military-aid-centric posture without any-mediated dialogue, this assumption fails. A reversal would be the emergence of a new, non-US-led mediation group (e.g., involving China or Turkey) that gains traction in the Kremlin's official communications.
  - *likely*

## Indicators & Warnings

- Increased frequency of drone-related casualties in Kyiv will signal a shift toward the 'High-Intensity' scenario.
  - The May 14 strike resulting in 24 deaths serves as a baseline for measuring the intensity of Russian kinetic operations. If casualty counts in Kyiv rise alongside the frequency of strikes, it undermines the possibility of a sustained US-brokered truce. A reversal would be a significant decrease in Russian strike frequency despite the expiration of the May 11 truce.
  - *weeks-months*
- Official Kremlin acknowledgment of the Moscow/Moscow region drone strikes would signal a shift toward the 'Rapid Escalation' scenario.
  - The current omission of the May 17 Moscow attack is a deliberate choice to avoid admitting vulnerability. If Peskov or other officials begin to explicitly name Moscow as a target of 'terrorist' drone strikes, it indicates the Kremlin is preparing the domestic population for a massive retaliatory response. This would be inconsistent with the current 'peace process' narrative. A reversal would be the continued, silent acceptance of Moscow-area incursions without official diplomatic protest.
  - *days-weeks*
- A sudden surge in US-led diplomatic summits involving both Russia and Ukraine would signal the 'US-led Mediation' scenario.
  - The presence of US officials in high-level, multi-party talks would contradict the current 'pause' in the peace process mentioned by Peskov. This would indicate that the May 9-11 truce was a precursor to a more permanent arrangement rather than an isolated event. A reversal would be the complete cessation of US-Russia diplomatic communication regarding the conflict.
  - *months*

## What to Watch For

Monitor Kremlin press briefings for any shift in the narrative regarding the May 17 Moscow drone strikes; an official acknowledgment would indicate a breakdown in the current information control strategy. Watch for changes in US diplomatic activity following the May 11 truce expiration to determine if the mediation framework is being abandoned. A signal that would challenge the current assessment of a 'pause' in negotiations would be the deployment of new, heavy-payload long-range drone assets by Ukraine specifically targeting Russian energy infrastructure.
