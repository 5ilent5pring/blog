The Russian Ministry of Digital Development, led by Maksut Shadayev, enforced a mandate in April requiring platforms to block VPN-enabled traffic under threat of losing IT accreditation. This regulatory action directly caused a 10% drop in Wildberries mobile traffic and significant revenue losses for sellers on Ozon and Yandex Market. The policy functions as a domestic economic tax, forcing Russian digital services to choose between regulatory compliance and the loss of a highly mobile, technologically-literate user base.

## Stakeholder Analysis

- Major Russian marketplaces like Wildberries and Ozon will prioritize regulatory compliance over user retention to protect their legal standing.
  - The Ministry of Digital Development threatened to remove companies from 'white lists' and revoke IT accreditation, a move that would strip these firms of critical tax and operational benefits. The observed 10% drop in Wildberries traffic following the April deadline demonstrates that these firms are actively implementing the restrictions despite the clear revenue impact.
  - *almost certainly*
- The Association of Internet Trade Companies will utilize economic loss data to lobby for regulatory exemptions.
  - The association has already publicly stated that VPN restrictions make it harder for services to reach customers abroad. They possess the data necessary to quantify the 7 billion ruble loss, providing the empirical basis for a campaign to decouple 'user data security' from 'VPN blocking' in future policy debates.
  - *likely*

## Key Assumptions Check

- The Ministry of Digital Development's stated goal of 'user data security' is a secondary justification for a primary goal of information control.
  - The timing of the April 15 deadline and the subsequent threat to IT accreditation suggest the policy is a tool for enforcing state-aligned internet architecture rather than a technical response to data breaches. If the Ministry introduces specific, documented technical vulnerabilities in VPN protocols as the driver for this mandate, my assessment would reverse.
  - *likely*
- The current VPN-blocking mandate will fail to permanently decouple Russian platforms from international users.
  - The 1.5% to 10% traffic fluctuations in April represent an initial shock, but the existence of the 'harder' access mentioned by the Association of Internet Trade Companies implies that users will seek new, more obfuscated circumvention methods. If traffic levels on Ozon and Yandex Market return to March growth levels within the next two quarters, my assessment would reverse.
  - *even odds*

## Second-Order Effects Analysis

- The policy will drive Russian e-commerce sellers toward decentralized or non-Russian-hosted payment and logistics workarounds.
  - With sellers reporting revenue losses up to 30%, the economic pressure will force a migration of transaction flows toward platforms that do not enforce the Ministry's VPN-blocking mandates. This creates a fragmented digital market where the state loses visibility into the total volume of domestic-linked commerce.
  - *likely*
- The mandate will accelerate the development of more resilient, protocol-obfuscating VPN technologies within the Russian domestic market.
  - By targeting the application layer (blocking access at the platform level), the state inadvertently incentivizes the creation of tools that mask the very 'VPN' signature the Ministry is attempting to eliminate. This creates a technical arms race that makes future domestic internet regulation more difficult and expensive.
  - *almost certainly*

## Indicators & Warnings

- A sudden increase in the revocation of IT accreditation for major Russian tech firms would confirm the Ministry is prioritizing political compliance over economic stability.
  - Monitoring the Ministry of Digital Development's official registry for changes in the status of companies like Yandex or Ozon over the next months will reveal if the 'white list' threat is being actively deployed as a punitive measure.
  - *months-quarters*
- The emergence of new, unnamed 'shadow' marketplaces with high growth in the Russian-speaking segment would indicate a successful evasion of the VPN-blocking mandate.
  - If traffic to established platforms like Wildberries continues to decline while unindexed or non-accredited platforms see surges, it confirms that the policy is successfully driving users toward unmonitored digital environments.
  - *months-quarters*
- A stabilization or rebound in Wildberries and Ozon mobile traffic in May or June would disconfirm the assessment that the VPN-blocking mandate is causing a permanent structural decline in user engagement.
  - If the April traffic drop is followed by a return to the March growth trends (e.g., Ozon's 10% month-over-month growth), it would suggest that the user base is successfully adapting to the new restrictions without abandoning the platforms.
  - *weeks-months*

## What to Watch For

Monitor the Ministry of Digital Development's regulatory actions regarding IT accreditation for Yandex and Ozon over the next three months. A signal to watch for is the sudden appearance of new, non-accredited digital marketplaces that bypass the April 15 restrictions, which would indicate the policy is failing to contain the user base. Conversely, if the 10% traffic drop in Wildberries persists through the next quarter, it confirms the policy is successfully degrading the reach of the Russian digital ecosystem.
