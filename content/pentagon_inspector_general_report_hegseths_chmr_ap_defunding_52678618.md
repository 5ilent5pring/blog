The Department of War Inspector General has documented a systemic failure to implement the 2022 Civilian Harm Mitigation and Response Action Plan (CHMR-AP) due to aggressive budget and personnel cuts under Secretary Pete Hegseth. These reductions include the termination of the CHMR data management platform and the reassignment of critical personnel at the Center of Excellence. The loss of verifiable strike data creates a strategic vacuum where the U.S. military can no longer independently corroborate or refute allegations of civilian casualties in active theaters like Iran.

## Stakeholder Analysis

- The Department of War leadership is prioritizing immediate budgetary retrenchment over long-term legal compliance and operational transparency.
  - The Inspector General found that the Department failed to implement any of the 11 CHMR-AP objectives by the end of FY 2025, specifically by ending funding for the data management platform. This suggests a deliberate move to reduce the administrative burden of civilian harm tracking. This assessment would be reversed if future IG reports show the failure was due to resource scarcity rather than active de-funding of existing platforms.
  - *almost certainly*
- U.S. Combatant Commands, specifically Southern Command, face increased operational friction due to the loss of casualty verification capabilities.
  - The article notes Southern Command is already unable to manage the volume of civilian casualty reports, and Joint Staff officials state that removing CHMR personnel increases the risk of failed strikes and local resistance. Their primary interest is maintaining mission success and battlefield awareness. This assessment would be reversed if Southern Command successfully implements a new, decentralized verification mechanism without the central Center of Excellence.
  - *likely*
- Congressional oversight bodies like the House Armed Services Committee will utilize budgetary leverage to force CHMR reinstatement.
  - Rep. Sara Jacobs is actively framing these cuts as a threat to national security and a violation of federal law to mobilize legislative resistance. The committee holds the power to mandate funding for the CHMR-AP in the upcoming fiscal year budget. This assessment would be reversed if the committee fails to include specific CHMR-AP line items in the next defense authorization.
  - *likely*

## Key Assumptions Check

- The primary driver for the CHMR-AP dismantlement is a strategic intent to reduce the legal and political accountability of U.S. kinetic operations.
  - The removal of the data management platform and the 'whitewashing' of the Center of Excellence suggest that the administration seeks to eliminate the technical means of verifying civilian harm. This allows for a lack of corroboration in theaters like Iran. This assessment would be reversed if evidence emerges that the data platform was decommissioned due to technical obsolescence or security vulnerabilities rather than budget cuts.
  - *likely*
- The reduction in CHMR capabilities will directly lead to an increase in asymmetric propaganda victories for U.S. adversaries.
  - The Inspector General and Joint Staff officials explicitly state that the loss of verification tools creates opportunities for adversaries to use unverified civilian harm incidents to undermine U.S. legitimacy. This creates a vacuum where uncorroborated reports of strikes on hospitals and schools can be leveraged globally. This assessment would be reversed if U.S. adversaries fail to utilize these specific incidents in their strategic communications or if the U.S. develops alternative, non-DoW verification methods.
  - *almost certainly*

## Second-Order Effects Analysis

- The erosion of CHMR capabilities will degrade the stability of U.S. military coalitions and international alliances.
  - The loss of transparent, verifiable data makes it politically difficult for allied nations to participate in U.S.-led kinetic operations, as they cannot guarantee compliance with international humanitarian law. This creates a diplomatic rift where partners may withdraw support to avoid being associated with unverified civilian casualties. This assessment would be reversed if allies develop their own independent, parallel verification frameworks that remain interoperable with U.S. operations.
  - *likely*
- The policy creates a self-reinforcing cycle of increased local resistance and prolonged conflict duration.
  - By removing the ability to respond to and mitigate civilian harm, the Department of War increases the likelihood of local populations engaging in kinetic resistance against U.S. forces. This directly contradicts the stated goal of mission success by expanding the theater of conflict. This assessment would be reversed if the reduction in CHMR personnel is accompanied by a significant decrease in the overall frequency of U.S. kinetic strikes.
  - *likely*

## Indicators & Warnings

- The emergence of unverified, high-profile reports of U.S. strikes on protected infrastructure (hospitals/schools) in Iran or other theaters will signal the failure of the current mitigation strategy.
  - The commander in Iran has already stated the U.S. has no means to corroborate such reports. A surge in these specific, unverified claims within the next 3-6 months would confirm that the lack of a data management platform is creating a verification crisis. This would DISCONFIRM the idea that the cuts are merely administrative and instead prove they are operationally damaging.
  - *weeks-months*
- The Department of War's decision to re-fund the CHMR data management platform or restore Center of Excellence billets in the next budget cycle will signal a reversal of the current policy trajectory.
  - If the upcoming fiscal year budget includes specific, non-zero allocations for the CHMR-AP objectives, it would indicate that Congressional pressure or internal military resistance has successfully checked the Secretary's authority. This would DISCONFIRM the assessment that the administration is committed to a permanent dismantlement of the CHMR enterprise.
  - *months-quarters*
