Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard has appointed Dave Mastro and James Cangialosi to lead a centralized interagency effort to monitor the 2026 election cycle. This consolidation follows the scaling back of the Foreign Malign Influence Center and significant personnel reductions within CISA. The centralization of intelligence oversight within the ODNI, paired with a weakened CISA, creates a structural bottleneck that prevents actionable threat intelligence from reaching the local election officials responsible for physical and digital infrastructure security.

## Stakeholder Analysis

- State and local election officials face increased vulnerability due to the loss of CISA's technical assistance capabilities.
  - The reduction of CISA's workforce by over one-third removes the primary mechanism for delivering digital security training and technical support to the ground level. This loss of capability forces local officials to rely on a centralized ODNI model that lacks a proven distribution framework for localized technical alerts. An increase in reported successful unauthorized access attempts at the county level would reverse this assessment.
  - *almost certainly*
- The ODNI's new election threats executive role seeks to centralize intelligence authority to control the narrative of foreign interference.
  - By appointing Mastro and Cangialosi to coordinate work across spy agencies, the ODNI is consolidating the evaluation and publication of foreign meddling evidence. This move follows the scaling back of the Foreign Malign Influence Center, suggesting a shift from specialized disinformation monitoring to a broader, more centralized intelligence oversight. Evidence of the ODNI creating new, independent disinformation-monitoring units would reverse this assessment.
  - *likely*

## Key Assumptions Check

- The ODNI's centralized monitoring model will fail to mitigate the impact of AI-driven disinformation during the 2026 cycle.
  - The policy assumes that intelligence collection by the ODNI is sufficient to counter threats, even as the technical distribution arm (CISA) is decimated. The rapid evolution of AI-generated deepfakes, as noted by Senator Mark Warner, allows adversaries to bypass centralized detection by flooding local information ecosystems faster than the ODNI can process and notify officials. The emergence of a highly effective, automated, and verified alert-sharing platform between the ODNI and local officials would reverse this assessment.
  - *likely*
- The primary driver for the ODNI reorganization is the mitigation of foreign adversary aggression rather than domestic political realignment.
  - The administration justifies the reorganization as a response to more aggressive foreign adversaries, a claim supported by House Intelligence Chair Rick Crawford. However, the simultaneous scaling back of the Foreign Malign Influence Center and the reduction of CISA personnel align with domestic political arguments regarding free speech and budget efficiency. Evidence that the reorganization resulted in increased funding and personnel for disinformation-specific units would reverse this assessment.
  - *even odds*

## Second-Order Effects Analysis

- The intelligence gap created by CISA's downsizing will drive state-level officials to seek private-sector cybersecurity partnerships.
  - As CISA's election security training and digital assistance diminish, local jurisdictions will likely contract private security firms to fill the technical void. This creates a fragmented security landscape where the quality of election integrity depends on local budgets rather than a unified national standard. A surge in state-level legislation mandating standardized, government-led security audits would reverse this assessment.
  - *likely*
- The centralization of election threat intelligence will trigger increased scrutiny and litigation from domestic political actors regarding censorship.
  - The consolidation of influence-monitoring capabilities into the ODNI provides a single point of failure for claims of domestic political bias. If the ODNI uses its centralized authority to label domestic speech as 'foreign-aligned,' it will likely face legal challenges and congressional investigations similar to those faced by the Foreign Malign Influence Center. A period of bipartisan consensus on the ODNI's notification framework would reverse this assessment.
  - *likely*

## Indicators & Warnings

- The deployment of large-scale, AI-generated deepfake campaigns targeting specific congressional districts will signal the failure of the ODNI's new coordination model.
  - If localized disinformation campaigns emerge in the months leading up to the 2026 midterms without corresponding ODNI-led warnings to local officials, it proves the distribution model is broken. This would confirm the assessment that the intelligence bottleneck is a critical vulnerability. Conversely, the rapid, coordinated identification and debunking of these deepfakes by local officials using ODNI data would disconfirm this assessment.
  - *months-quarters*
- The reactivation of the joint US Cyber Command and NSA election security group will signal a return to technical-centric defense.
  - The proposal to send plans for this group to the ODNI and Defense Secretary Hegseth suggests a move toward reintegrating technical defense with intelligence oversight. If this group begins publishing technical indicators of compromise (IOCs) related to election infrastructure within the next year, it would disconfirm the assessment that the defense posture is purely focused on intelligence-led narrative management.
  - *weeks-months*

## What to Watch For

Monitor the ODNI's upcoming proposals to Secretary Hegseth regarding the joint Cyber Command/NSA election security group over the next few months; a failure to formalize this group would confirm a breakdown in technical defense. Watch for any increase in unauthorized access reports from local election offices in the 2026 cycle, which would indicate that the CISA personnel reductions have successfully severed the intelligence-to-action pipeline.
