The Pentagon's recent deployment of the Maven Smart System (MSS) during the 38-day air war against Iran represents a fundamental shift from experimental AI to high-volume kinetic targeting. While the 4,425 percent surge in token usage demonstrates the massive scale of data processing required for 13,000 targets, the operational reliance on Palantir-built tools creates a new strategic vulnerability centered on compute availability rather than adversary interception. The Pentagon's primary operational bottleneck is now the physical capacity to scale hardware to meet the 'insatiable appetite' of automated target identification.

## Scenario Analysis

- The conflict enters a period of sustained, high-intensity automated attrition.
  - The US maintains the current trajectory of using MSS to process massive datasets for continuous strikes against Iranian infrastructure. This relies on the Pentagon's ability to expand compute capacity to match the 20 billion token daily peaks seen in Operation Epic Fury. A second-order consequence is the exhaustion of specialized hardware supply chains. The cyber dimension involves the protection of the massive data pipelines feeding the MSS from Iranian disruption.
  - *most likely*
- The conflict shifts toward a localized, low-intensity containment strategy.
  - US commanders intentionally throttle the use of high-token-count automated agents to avoid the intelligence errors that led to the schoolyard casualties near the IRGC base. This decision reduces the 'insatiable appetite' for compute but increases the cognitive load on human analysts. The cyber dimension focuses on traditional signal intelligence rather than large-scale automated processing.
  - *less likely but significant*
- A rapid escalation occurs driven by automated algorithmic error.
  - A failure in the 'human-machine team' leads to an automated strike on a high-value non-combatant target, triggering a massive Iranian retaliatory strike. This would be driven by the inability of human operators to provide 'operational art' at the speed of the 4,425 percent surge in processing. The cyber dimension involves the potential for Iranian actors to inject adversarial data into the MSS pipeline to trigger such errors.
  - *low probability but high impact*

## Key Assumptions Check

- The Pentagon can expand compute capacity fast enough to sustain current strike volumes.
  - The CDAO's stated fear is the inability to keep up with the demand for processing power. If the Department fails to secure the necessary hardware and energy infrastructure, the US will be forced to reduce the scale of its air operations, effectively capping its kinetic reach. I would reverse this assessment if the Pentagon announces a major new procurement of sovereign, high-density compute clusters or a shift to edge-computing architectures that bypass centralized bottlenecks.
  - *even odds*
- The integration of 'low-code/no-code' tools by non-specialist troops maintains target accuracy.
  - The article notes that 'amateurs' are building their own software applications using MSS. If these unvetted applications lack the 'legal intent' and 'context' mentioned by Stanley, the error rate in target identification will rise. I would reverse this assessment if post-conflict audits show a decrease in collateral damage despite the increased use of user-generated AI agents.
  - *unlikely*

## Indicators & Warnings

- A shift in US targeting from mobile assets to fixed infrastructure indicates a move toward the 'containment' scenario.
  - A reduction in the daily token count or a drop in the 20 billion token peak would suggest the US is moving away from the high-intensity, wide-area surveillance required for mobile target tracking. This would undermine the 'sustained attrition' scenario and support the 'low-intensity' scenario.
  - *weeks-months*
- Iranian cyber operations target the underlying data ingestion layers of the MSS.
  - Evidence of sophisticated data poisoning or denial-of-service attacks against the unclassified and classified data streams used by Palantir's system would indicate an attempt to force the 'automated error' escalation scenario. This would undermine the 'sustained attrition' scenario by making the high-volume use of MSS too risky to maintain.
  - *days-weeks*
- A sudden surge in US deployment of specialized electronic warfare units to the region.
  - This would be a surprising indicator that the US is preparing for a scenario where the 'compute' bottleneck is being addressed through localized, hardened infrastructure rather than just scaling centralized cloud capacity. This would challenge the assumption that the primary threat is purely a lack of raw processing power.
  - *weeks*

## What to Watch For

Monitor Pentagon procurement announcements regarding high-density compute and energy infrastructure over the next 3-6 months to assess the sustainability of the current strike tempo. Watch for any reports of significant civilian casualties from US strikes, as this would indicate a failure in the 'human-machine' oversight and a potential shift toward the high-impact escalation scenario. A lack of increase in token usage despite continued operations would signal a critical failure in the US ability to scale its AI-enabled C2.
