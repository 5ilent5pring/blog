The Joint Electromagnetic Spectrum Operations Center (JEC) recently conducted 'Aurora Pulse,' a tabletop exercise involving senior leaders from US Northern Command, Cyber Command, and the Intelligence Community. This shift from tactical staff drills to strategic-level decision-making by J3s and J5s indicates a transition toward preparing for high-end, contested spectrum warfare in the Arctic. The exercise reveals that US command structures are actively re-eval्यताing their reliance on unencrypted or permissive spectrum access, specifically targeting the mitigation of adversary-driven signal denial in extreme environments.

## Scenario Analysis

- A localized Arctic spectrum denial event will drive US command restructuring toward decentralized, autonomous communication protocols.
  - Adversaries will use electronic warfare to disrupt US communications in the Arctic, forcing Northern Command and Cyber Command to implement the 'tweaks to TTPs' mentioned by Maj. Gen. Anthony. This will lead to a permanent shift in how J3/J5 planners integrate spectrum management into mission planning. A reversal would occur if US forces successfully deploy large-scale, un-disruptable satellite constellations that render local spectrum denial ineffective.
  - *most likely*
- Persistent spectrum congestion in the Arctic will trigger a shift from electronic warfare to kinetic-cyber hybrid operations.
  - As adversaries prove 'adept' at denying the spectrum, US forces may move from reactive TTP adjustments to proactive disruption of adversary electronic warfare infrastructure. This scenario relies on the identified need for improved coordination between Cyber Command and Northern Command. This assessment is invalidated if the JEC's future modeling and simulation shows that spectrum denial can be mitigated purely through software-defined radio advancements without kinetic or offensive cyber intervention.
  - *less likely but significant*
- A total spectrum blackout in the Arctic leads to a strategic failure of US maritime and troop positioning.
  - The inability to maintain precise munitions and troop location data, as identified by Anthony, would result in a loss of freedom of action during a crisis. This would force a rapid, unplanned deployment of electronic warfare assets to the region. This is unlikely to occur if the 'valuable insights' from Aurora Pulse successfully result in the deployment of resilient, low-probability-of-intercept communication systems before a conflict begins.
  - *low probability but high impact*

## Key Assumptions Check

- US military effectiveness in the Arctic is fundamentally dependent on maintaining a permissive electromagnetic environment.
  - The exercise design assumes that current US reliance on the spectrum for 'everything from communication to precise munitions' creates a critical vulnerability. If US forces have already developed sufficient 'silent' or low-signature operational modes that do not rely on high-bandwidth, detectable spectrum, the strategic urgency of Aurora Pulse is overstated. Evidence of successful, large-scale silent operations in recent Arctic exercises would reverse this.
  - *almost certainly*
- Adversary electronic warfare capabilities are currently capable of disrupting US command and control (C2) at the strategic level.
  - The involvement of J3s and J5s from Strategic Command and Indo-Pacific Command suggests the JEC believes spectrum denial can impact global-scale operations. If adversary capabilities are actually limited to localized, tactical-level jamming that does not threaten the C2 of theater-level commanders, the strategic focus of this exercise is misplaced. Evidence of adversary jamming being easily bypassed by standard US electronic counter-countermeasures (ECCM) would reverse this.
  - *likely*

## Indicators & Warnings

- Increased deployment of electronic warfare (EW) sensing assets by US Northern Command indicates a shift toward the 'Localized Denial' scenario.
  - The movement of specialized spectrum-monitoring hardware into Arctic stations would signal that the US is actively implementing the TTP changes identified in Aurora Pulse. This would be inconsistent with the 'Total Blackout' scenario, which assumes a lack of-preparedness. This indicator is supported by the JEC's stated goal of improving spectrum management.
  - *weeks-months*
- Adversary deployment of high-power, wide-band jamming platforms in the Arctic indicates an escalation toward the 'Hybrid Operations' scenario.
  - The appearance of mobile, high-output electronic warfare units near US Arctic interests would signal that adversaries are testing the 'contested and congested' environment described by Anthony. This would be inconsistent with a status-quo environment where the spectrum remains relatively permissive. This indicator would surprise me if it occurred alongside a simultaneous increase in US diplomatic de-escalation efforts.
  - *months*

## What to Watch For

Monitor the JEC's future 'modeling and simulation' outputs for any shift toward quantitative metrics of spectrum degradation. Watch for changes in US Northern Command's procurement of spectrum-resilient communication hardware over the next 12-18 months. A sudden increase in US-led Arctic maritime exercises that do not involve electronic warfare components would challenge the assessment that the US views the spectrum as an actively contested space.
